# Talk: Grandma's lentil soup

A 3-minute hackathon pitch for a tool that finds business rules hidden in code. The product name
(Footnote), the rule and every number are invented for this example.

Product: Footnote
Limit: 3:00
Pace: 140
Avoid: left, gone, died
Keep: Grandma can retire. Her secrets stay.

## Ledger

| Real part | Story element | First beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The teammate who knows the unwritten rules | Grandma | 1 |
| The ticket: what was written down | The recipe card | 2 |
| The rule in the code: what really happens | The cumin goes in at the very end | 2 |
| The projects that use the rule | Four kitchens | 3 |
| What the logs show | Four bitter soups | 3 |
| What the product does | A new line on the card, in a second hand | 8 |
| How the product works inside | (plain) | 5 |

## Beats

### 1. Hook (10 s)
Line: Grandma = the teammate who knows the rules nobody wrote down.
Say: Raise your hand if your family has a recipe that never tastes the same when someone else
cooks it. (Raise your own hand. Wait two seconds.)

### 2. The secret (20 s)
Line: Grandma knew a secret she never wrote down.
Say: My grandma's lentil soup. The recipe card says one spoon of cumin. What the card does not
say: she added it at the very end. Cumin that cooks too long turns bitter. She never wrote that
down. Why would she? She was always there.

### 3. The loss (20 s)
Line: When she retired, nobody knew her secret.
Say: Then Grandma retired to the seaside. Four of us cook from her card now. Four kitchens, four
bitter soups. We blame the lentils. We blame the pot. We blame the stove. Nobody blames the card,
because the card is right.

### 4. The turn (25 s)
Line: Our problem: important knowledge lives only in people's heads.
Say: Our code has grandmas too. The ticket is the recipe card: it says what we agreed. The code
is the cumin at the very end: it does what someone knew. And when that someone retires, the logs
fill up with bitter soup. (From here on, talk plainly about code. Grandma comes back at the close.)

### 5. The answer (20 s)
Line: Footnote writes that knowledge down before anyone retires.
Say: Footnote does four jobs. It finds the rules hidden in the code. It maps which projects use
each rule. It warns when the ticket and the code disagree. And when a rule breaks, it searches
the logs and names the most likely cause.

### 6. The peak: live demo (55 s)
Line: One rule changed. Footnote finds it and says why.
Say: Here is a rule: refunds within thirty days. I change one line. Thirty becomes three.
(Change it. Stay quiet until the answer is on screen.) Now I ask Footnote what broke. It found
the rule. It shows the three projects that use it. It shows the ticket that still says thirty.
And it shows the first refund that was refused. Most likely cause: this one line.
(Plan B is a recording. Introduce it with: here is the same run from this morning.)

### 7. Proof (10 s)
Line: [X] of 10 planted breaks found, [Y] false alarms.
Say: We planted ten breaks in a test project. Footnote found [X] of them, with [Y] false alarms.

### 8. The end (15 s)
Line: Grandma can retire. Her secrets stay.
Say: (Hold up the real recipe card.) There is a new line on the card now, in a second hand: add
the cumin at the end, or it turns bitter. Grandma can retire. Her secrets stay.
